Mashed Potatoes in the Body

The Story

This section will include how the mashed potatoes travel through the body and are digested.

When the mouth chews and breaks down mashed potatoes, it is turned into a soft moist ball called a bolus. In the saliva, amylase breaks down the starches in the mashed potatoes into sugar The esophagus then moves the bolus down itself using peristalsis. (rhythmic smooth muscle contractions) The lower esophageal sphincter, which is a ring-like valve, relaxes to allow the mashed potatoes to slide in, and then closes to prevent acid from backing up When the bolus drops into the stomach, it acts like a mixer and blender; the stomach’s contractions churn the food while gastric juices, hydrochloric acid, and enzymes (like pepsin) dissolve it and break down proteins. The bolus transforms into a thick, frothy liquid called chyme, which slowly empties into the small intestine. The small intestine is the main site for digestion and nutrient absorption. Chyme mixes with digestive enzymes from the pancreas and bile from the liver/gallbladder to break down fats, proteins, and carbohydrates. Tiny, finger-like projections called villi line the walls and absorb nutrients directly into the bloodstream. Leftover waste, water, and fiber move onward. The large intestine absorbs most of the remaining water and electrolytes from the undigested food material, while resident bacteria break down leftover bits. The liquid waste gradually solidifies into stool (feces) over the course of about 30 to 40 hours. The lower end of the large intestine, called the rectum, acts as a storage site. Nerves in the rectum sense when it is full and signal the body that it is time for a bowel movement. In the anus, Internal and external muscular sphincters control the release of stool. Feces are expelled from the body, completing the journey.

The Two Types of Digestion

There are two types of digestion, mechanical digestion and chemical digestion. Mechanical Digestion happens in the Mouth, the Esophagus, the Pharynx, the Stomach, and the Small Intestine. Chemical Digestion happens in the Mouth with Saliva, the Stomach, and Small Intestine.

What is Hydrolisis

Hydrolysis is a chemical reaction where a water molecule is consumed to break a covalent bond inside a large molecule (polymer), splitting it into smaller components (monomers or smaller subunits). Mashed potatoes don’t have many polymers; therefore hydrolysis isn’t used when breaking it down.

Enzymes +Other

  • Amylase
  • Pepsin
  • Bile

Amylase is produced in the salivary glands and is inside of your saliva. It is used to break down starch into more simple carbohydrates. Pepsin is an enzyme that is activated by low ph inside of the stomach. To activate the Pepsin, the Stomach uses Hydrochloric Acid to lower the ph inside of the Stomach. Bile is used to break down fat in the Small Intestine and it is released by the Gall Bladder.
